The Challenge

A Specialist Competing Against Everyone

A periodontics and implant practice has a structural disadvantage in search. The patient does not wake up looking for a periodontist — they wake up with a gum receding on one tooth, or a tooth that suddenly feels like it is moving. They search the symptom. The pages that answer those searches belong to general dentists, hospital systems and national content mills with far bigger sites and far more links.

OC Perio & Implants had the clinical credentials — three board-certified specialists, two of them international lecturers in implantology and periodontics — and almost no organic reach to match. In the three months to 24 August 2025 the site drew 557 organic clicks from 168,032 impressions: a 0.33% click-through rate. Google was showing the site plenty; it just wasn’t showing it high enough to be clicked.

The commercial keywords told the same story. In the local Mission Viejo SERP the practice sat at position 28 for “dental implants near me” and position 29 for “dental implant center near me” — page three, invisible in practice. And more than half of the search traffic came from people who already knew the practice by name — patients it had, not patients it was winning. The site was serving the patients it already had, not finding new ones.

Ranking well for “periodontist” is not the hard part. Being found by the patient who does not yet know they need one — that is the hard part.

The Process

How We Built It

Five workstreams over twelve months. The technical and local work made the practice competitive for the commercial implant and periodontist keywords; the content work made it findable for the clinical questions that come first.

1. Site Speed and Technical Health Audit

Most patients find a specialist on a phone, often in the waiting room of the dentist who just referred them. So the first thing we measure is what that patient experiences — not how the site looks on a desktop in the office. Clinical photography and long treatment pages make a specialty site heavy, and a slow page loses the patient before a word of it is read.

  • Measured load speed on a phone for the homepage and every treatment page
  • Compressed the clinical image library so pages load fast without losing detail
  • Removed the scripts and add-ons that were costing speed and earning nothing
  • Found and fixed the broken links and dead ends patients and Google were hitting
  • Set a speed benchmark we report on monthly, so it never quietly slips back

A fast site does not win on its own — but a slow one wastes everything you spend on it. Fixing this first meant every improvement that followed actually counted.

2. Technical SEO Foundation

The practice had already invested in content — dozens of treatment pages, a patient FAQ library, a blog. That breadth is an asset, but only if Google can tell which page answers which question. We found the pattern we see in almost every specialty practice: several pages quietly competing with each other, and the pages that actually book cases buried the deepest.

  • Rewrote every treatment page to use the words patients actually type, not clinical terminology
  • Gave each treatment one clear page to own — implants, gum grafting, LANAP, All-on-4, Perioscope
  • Connected every article to the treatment it leads to, so readers land where they can book
  • Made the practice, the three doctors and each procedure legible to Google and to AI search
  • Cleaned up years of old and duplicate pages so nothing competed with the pages that matter

Google already knew the practice was relevant — it was showing the site 168,000 times a quarter. This work told it which page deserved to be at the top.

3. Local SEO and Google Maps Optimization

This is where a specialty practice wins or loses the commercially valuable searches. Someone typing “dental implants near me” in Mission Viejo is ready to book, and Google answers them with a map pack and a locally-weighted set of results. Those rankings are decided city by city — which is exactly why we measure them in a location-specific tracker rather than reading a national average.

  • Rebuilt the Google Business Profile: full procedure list, specialist categories, service areas and Q&A
  • Corrected name, address and phone consistency across local and dental-industry directories
  • Strengthened location signals tying the practice to Mission Viejo and the surrounding Orange County cities
  • Built out nearby-city implant pages — Irvine, Laguna Niguel, San Clemente, Aliso Viejo, Laguna Hills
  • Ran an ongoing review strategy to hold map-pack position against larger multi-location competitors

Twelve months on, eight priority keywords sit in the local top 5 — five of them in the top 3, and two at #1, including the highest-volume implant search in the set.

4. Content Strategy for Search Intent

Here is the engine. A patient with early gum recession does not search “periodontist Mission Viejo” — they search “why is my gum receding on one tooth.” A patient told they need an extraction searches “what happens if I don’t get a bone graft.” These are the questions that come before the patient knows a specialist exists, and they were being answered by everyone except the specialists.

So we built the pages that answer them properly — written to a clinical standard, structured for the way Google reads a question page, and each one linked into the treatment it leads to. The gum-recession article carries causes, warning signs, why recession does not reverse on its own, and the actual treatment options: gum grafting and the Chao Pinhole technique. It ends where it should — with a consultation.

  • Mapped the clinical questions patients search before diagnosis, grouped by the procedure each one leads to
  • Built long-form answer pages, one per question cluster, reviewed against the doctors’ clinical guidance
  • Linked every article into its destination treatment page — grafting, bone regeneration, implants, LANAP
  • Added FAQ blocks targeting the specific phrasings patients type, including misspellings and variants
  • Closed every page with a booking path rather than leaving the reader at the end of an article

Four pages that started from zero now bring in more than 3,200 visits a quarter between them. And the balance has flipped: where most search traffic once came from people who already knew the practice, the overwhelming majority now comes from patients searching for treatment. That is new case flow, not the existing patient list looking up a phone number.

5. Link Building and Authority Growth

To outrank a general dentist, a specialist has to look more credible to Google, not just more relevant. Credibility comes largely from which other respected sites reference yours — and for surgical and health topics, Google sets that bar higher than anywhere else.

  • Earned coverage in dental and health publications that carry real weight in the field
  • Corrected and built the practice listings across local, medical and dental directories
  • Put the doctors’ real credentials to work — board certification and international lecturing open doors most practices cannot
  • Directed that new credibility to the pages already winning patients
  • Reviewed the profile every month — every link earned was relevant, clean and permanent

Referring sites grew 55% and total links 87% in twelve months. That is what moved the practice past general-dentistry competitors in its own market — the only market that sends it patients.
