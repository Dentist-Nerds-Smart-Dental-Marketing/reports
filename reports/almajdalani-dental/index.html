Issues Ranked by Impact

The 15 issues, in priority order

Each issue includes evidence from the audit crawl and a recommended fix. None of these is a site-breaking emergency — the site works. They're the gap between a functional website and one that competes for every "Daly City" and "San Francisco" search the practice should be winning. Together they make up the full to-do list for the site.

📝
High Thin Service Pages Issue #01

Service pages lack the depth to rank or convert

The full-mouth-reconstruction page runs roughly 800 words of general, definition-style copy — no FAQs, no before/after gallery (only image placeholders), no cost or financing detail on the page itself, and no patient testimonials. The implants, orthodontics, Invisalign, endodontics, and whitening pages follow the same thin template. To rank in search and surface in AI answers, a service page needs real depth.

📌 Evidence

/specialty/full-mouth-reconstruction — ~800 words · 0 FAQs · 0 testimonials · image placeholders only · no on-page cost guidance. Every /specialty/ page draws 0 organic visits.

✓ The fix

Rebuild each service page to 1,000–1,500 words of practice-specific content with 5–8 procedure FAQs, embedded testimonials drawn from existing reviews, a real before/after gallery, cost or financing guidance (Cherry / Sunbit), and a prominent above-the-fold "Book a Consultation" CTA.

🎯
High Geo-Targeting Issue #02

On-page targeting is spread across cities the practice doesn't serve

The full-mouth-reconstruction page ranks for that procedure in Walnut Creek, Roseville, Napa, Oakland, Los Altos, Moraga, Century City, and Park City — all well outside the practice's Daly City / San Francisco market — but only modestly for its actual home turf. The page is trying to rank everywhere and converting nowhere. None of those far-flung rankings sends real patients.

📌 Evidence · ranking keywords for one page

full mouth reconstruction walnut creek (#70) · roseville (#86) · los altos (#54) · napa ca (#65) · oakland (#53) · moraga (#56) · century city (#87). Local "san francisco" / "sf" variants rank #9–#15.

✓ The fix

Refocus each service page on the practice's real geography — Daly City, San Francisco (Marina), and the immediate Peninsula. Build dedicated, locally written landing pages for the neighborhoods the practice actually serves rather than chasing distant metros from a single generic page.

📊
High Traffic Concentration Issue #03

25 pages are published — only one earns any traffic

The site has 25 published pages, but just 9 earn any ranking at all and only the homepage earns organic traffic. The practice's entire search presence rests on one URL ranking for the doctor's name. There's no second or third page sharing the load — which caps total traffic and leaves the site exposed if homepage rankings ever move.

✓ The fix

Spread visibility across the existing pages — depth on the service pages (Issue #01), tighter local targeting (Issue #02), and new procedure and location pages — so each service earns its own rankings and traffic instead of leaning entirely on the homepage.

📢
High Title Tags Issue #04

Page titles are bloated and repeat themselves — site-wide

Every service page title runs about 140 characters and repeats its service name twice plus both ZIP codes. Google truncates titles around 60 characters, so the duplicated text never shows — and the repetition reads as keyword stuffing. The homepage title also pads the end with "94123 & 94015."

📌 Evidence · pattern repeats on every service page

"Full Mouth Reconstruction in Daly City & San Francisco, CA | Mouhannad Almajdalani, D.D.S., F.I.C.O.I., M.A.G.D. | Full Mouth Reconstruction in 94015 & 94123" — same double-phrasing on Invisalign, Endodontics, Orthodontics, etc.

✓ The fix

Rewrite every title under 60 characters using a clean pattern — "Full Mouth Reconstruction in San Francisco · Almajdalani DDS." One service, one primary city, the practice name. Drop the duplicate phrase and the ZIP-code padding.

🚧
High Placeholder Pages Issue #05

Placeholder pages shipped to production and got indexed

The practice's insurance content lives at the URL /untitled-3/ — a default placeholder slug that was never renamed. A second page at /page/ is live with no title and almost no content. Both are in the Google sitemap. Patients and search engines reading these URLs see a half-finished site, and the placeholder slugs waste the chance to rank for "insurance" and the content they hold.

📌 Evidence

/untitled-3/ → 200 OK · page title "insurance | Mouhannad Almajdalani…" (real insurance content on a placeholder URL). /page/ → 200 OK · no <title> · near-empty. Both listed in page-sitemap.xml.

✓ The fix

Move the insurance content to a descriptive URL (/insurance) and 301-redirect /untitled-3/. Build out or delete the empty /page/ URL and remove it from the sitemap.

🤖
Medium Schema Errors Issue #06

Schema is present but carries errors — and service pages have none

The homepage has valid Dentist schema with both locations and a 5.0 / 31-review AggregateRating — a real strength. But it contains bugs: a literal "description": "null" string, a lowercase contactPoint type that should be ContactPoint, and two conflicting opening-hours blocks (Mon–Fri 9–6 alongside Tue/Wed/Fri 10–5). The service pages carry only generic Service markup — no MedicalProcedure or FAQPage schema.

✓ The fix

Clean up the homepage schema (remove the "null" description, fix the ContactPoint casing, reconcile the hours), and add MedicalProcedure + FAQPage schema to each service page so procedures and FAQs can surface in rich results and AI answers.

📉
Medium Analytics Issue #07

No live analytics — still running deprecated Universal Analytics

The site loads a Universal Analytics tag (UA-109562209-2). Google shut Universal Analytics down in July 2023 — it stopped collecting data nearly three years ago. With no GA4 property in place, there's no current data on how visitors find the site, which pages they read, or where they drop off before booking.

✓ The fix

Install GA4 and connect Google Search Console, then set up conversion tracking for calls and appointment requests so every later improvement can be measured against a real baseline.

✍️
Medium Dormant Blog Issue #08

The blog has been abandoned since early 2024

The site's blog holds just two posts, and the post sitemap was last updated in January 2024 — roughly two and a half years ago. A dormant blog sends a "low activity" signal and forfeits the easiest way to capture the patient-question searches ("how much do implants cost in San Francisco," "is Invisalign worth it") that competitors pick up.

📌 Evidence

post-sitemap.xml — 2 URLs · lastmod 2024-01-05. No new posts in ~30 months.

✓ The fix

Restart a monthly blog cadence focused on real patient questions and Daly City / SF topics — seeded from the existing keywords and competitor gaps — so the site keeps adding ranking terms over time.

📦
Medium Shrinking Footprint Issue #09

The ranking keyword footprint is shrinking

Over the past 12 months the number of keywords the site ranks for has fallen from 70 to 45, and monthly organic traffic has drifted from a 171-visit high last autumn to 126 today. The trend is gentle, not a cliff — but it points the wrong way for a practice this established, and it's a direct symptom of the thin, static content and the dormant blog (Issues #01 and #08).

✓ The fix

Reverse the drift with a steady content cadence — deeper service pages plus the revived blog — so the site keeps adding ranking terms instead of slowly shedding them.

🔗
Medium Backlink Authority Issue #10

Backlink authority trails the stronger local practices

The site holds Authority Score 8 with 131 referring domains — a respectable base. But the strongest practice in the immediate market sits at AS 16 with 331 referring domains, and roughly half of this site's links (119 of 246) are nofollow, so they pass little ranking weight. The link gap is a meaningful part of why competitors out-rank the practice on shared commercial terms.

✓ The fix

Run a steady local-link program — San Francisco and Daly City directories, the dental societies, UCSF faculty and alumni mentions for Dr. Almajdalani, neighborhood and chamber partnerships — to grow legitimate referring domains over the next 6–12 months.

⚡
Medium Page Weight Issue #11

Heavy pages and inline images slow the site down

The homepage delivers about 260 KB of raw HTML with several large images embedded directly in the markup as base64 data. Inlining images this way inflates the page, blocks them from being cached, and hurts mobile load speed and Core Web Vitals — which both patients and Google's rankings respond to.

📌 Evidence

Homepage HTML ≈ 260 KB · 9 inline data:image base64 blocks · WordPress + GrowthPlug front-end.

✓ The fix

Serve images as cached, compressed files (WebP) with proper width/height and lazy-loading instead of inline base64. Run the pages through PageSpeed Insights and resolve the largest Core Web Vitals offenders.

📱
Low Mobile Accessibility Issue #12

Mobile zoom is disabled in the viewport tag

The page sets user-scalable=no in the viewport meta tag, which blocks pinch-to-zoom on phones. For an older patient base reading procedure and financing details on mobile, that's a real accessibility barrier — and it's flagged by accessibility (WCAG) checks.

✓ The fix

Remove user-scalable=no (and any maximum-scale limit) from the viewport tag so patients can zoom in on text and images.

🌐
Low URL Structure Issue #13

Generic /page/ slugs instead of descriptive paths

Several indexed URLs use generic slugs like /page/sunbit-financing/ and /page/about/ rather than clear, keyword-relevant paths. Descriptive URLs help both search engines and patients understand a page before they click it.

✓ The fix

Move content onto descriptive URLs (e.g. /financing/sunbit, /about) and 301-redirect the old /page/ paths so existing rankings and links carry over.

🔁
Low Redirects & Canonicals Issue #14

Two-hop redirect chain and missing canonical tags

Reaching the canonical address takes two redirects — http://almajdalanidental.com → https://almajdalanidental.com → https://www.almajdalanidental.com. Each hop loses a little speed and link equity. The homepage HTML also ships without a rel="canonical" tag, which can leave Google guessing the preferred URL.

📌 Evidence

http → 301 → https (non-www) → 301 → https://www … (2 hops). No rel="canonical" detected in the delivered homepage markup.

✓ The fix

Collapse the redirect to a single hop straight to https://www.almajdalanidental.com, and add a self-referencing canonical tag to every page.

🤖
Low robots.txt Issue #15

Malformed rule in robots.txt

The robots.txt file carries a garbled, repeated directive — Disallow: /suggest/?*suggest/?*suggest/?* — which isn't a valid rule. It does no harm today, but it signals an unmaintained file and should be cleaned up alongside the rest of the technical pass.

✓ The fix

Rewrite robots.txt with valid directives, confirm the sitemap reference points to sitemap_index.xml, and verify nothing important is being blocked.

Strategic Positioning

The brand entity isn't yet local-anchored

The practice's strongest asset in search is the doctor's name — it ranks #1. But the brand isn't tied to a place or a flagship service. Searchers looking for "implant dentist San Francisco" or "Daly City orthodontics" don't yet connect those needs to this practice, because the site presents itself as a doctor's name rather than a local, service-led brand.

Recommended positioning shift

From a doctor's name to a local, service-led entity

Dr. Almajdalani's 24 years in practice, UCSF faculty role, and implant credentials (FICOI, MAGD) are genuine authority signals — but they currently live mostly on the homepage. Pairing the doctor's name with a consistent local-practice identity across the site, schema, and citations makes the brand legible to both patients and search engines as the implant and full-mouth-reconstruction practice for Daly City and the SF Marina.
