Other Key Findings · What Needs Improvement

⚠
All treatment pages have thin content and no FAQs

Every service page — dental implants, cosmetic dentistry, veneers, Invisalign, sedation — contains minimal text with no FAQ sections. FAQs are critical for implant and cosmetic pages because patients have high anxiety and many questions before booking a consultation. Thin pages also fail to rank for long-tail queries that represent ready-to-book patients. Each treatment page needs 600–1,200 words of strategic content plus at minimum 5–8 FAQs structured with FAQ schema.

⚠
Treatment pages are using stock photography — replace with real patient imagery

Stock dental imagery signals an impersonal, generic practice experience to prospective patients — especially for implant and cosmetic patients who are evaluating multiple providers and making high-consideration decisions. Authentic before/after photos, photos of Dr. Takher at work, the actual office and team, and real patient success stories dramatically increase trust and conversion rates on treatment pages. This is a conversion issue, not just an aesthetic one.

⚠
Only 39 indexed pages — significant content expansion opportunity

A specialty implant and cosmetic practice should have 80–150+ indexed pages covering individual procedures (All-on-4, single implants, full-mouth reconstruction, implant-supported dentures), condition pages (missing teeth, bone loss), cosmetic procedures, financing, and location-targeted landing pages. Each missing page is a missed ranking opportunity for high-intent searches. Content expansion should be prioritized around the highest-value procedures.

⚠
No schema markup deployed — invisible to AI Overviews and rich results

With 904 reviews at 4.9 stars, the practice has an exceptional reputation signal that isn't surfacing in search results because there's no LocalBusiness, AggregateRating, MedicalProcedure, or FAQPage schema on any page. Google's AI Overviews, rich snippets, and the new AI Mode increasingly reward structured data. The review asset already exists — it just needs to be published in a format search engines can read and display.

⚠
Flat traffic trend — no growth in 12 months despite review growth

Traffic has oscillated between 247 and 552 visits per month over the last year with no consistent upward trend. The spike in August 2025 (552 visits) did not sustain, indicating it was likely a one-off referral event rather than organic growth. Without a content strategy producing new pages and targeting new keywords each month, traffic will remain flat while competitors grow their footprints.

Strategic direction

Website Strategy: Implant-Focused vs. General Dentistry

This is the most important strategic question for the website. The current structure positions dental implants prominently in the navigation (before the main services menu), but the website experience, content depth, and conversion design don't yet fully support an implants-first positioning. The right path depends on the practice's revenue goals — and the two directions require very different approaches.

Choose your direction

Two Strategic Paths — Each Requires a Different Design & Content Approach

Path A — Implants First

Design the entire website to convert implant patients

  • Implant conversion requires more trust-building psychology — patients need education, before/afters, cost transparency, and multiple touchpoints before booking
  • Homepage hero section speaks directly to implant patients — "Missing teeth? We specialize in dental implants."
  • Dedicated pages for All-on-4, single implants, implant-supported dentures, mini implants, and full-mouth reconstruction
  • Financing/cost page prominently displayed — implant patients always need to see payment options before calling
  • Patient video testimonials and real before/after gallery as primary conversion tools
  • FAQ-rich content addressing fear, cost, and timeline objections on every implant page
  • Navigation restructured: Dental Implants as first and primary menu item with its own mega-menu or dropdown
Path B — Full-Service General Dentistry

Organize around a broad service menu with implants as a specialty

  • Homepage speaks to the full family — routine care, preventive, cosmetic, and specialty services all represented
  • Navigation organized by patient need: "New Patients," "General Care," "Cosmetic," "Implants & Surgery"
  • Implants featured as a highlighted specialty within a broader service structure, not the lead
  • Content strategy covers a wider range of topics including preventive care, pediatric considerations, emergency dental, and cosmetic queries
  • Broader geographic keyword targeting possible — more search queries to target
  • Lower average case value per patient but higher volume potential
  • Conversion psychology is simpler — "book an appointment" works for most general dentistry patients
Our recommendation: If dental implants represent 40%+ of practice revenue or is the intended growth area, commit fully to Path A. The current halfway positioning — implants in the menu but without implant-specific conversion design — captures neither market effectively. A full implant-focused website redesign with the right psychology, content depth, and trust-builders could 3–5× new implant patient inquiries within 6–12 months. If the practice is pivoting toward higher volume general dentistry, Path B with a complete site reorganization is the right call. Discuss with your Dentist Nerds strategist which direction fits the practice growth plan.
