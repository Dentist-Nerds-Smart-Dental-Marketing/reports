Biggest Priorities

Top 3 Weaknesses

The three issues doing the most damage right now — fix these first and everything else compounds faster.

01

The site lost 78% of its keywords

In June 2023 this domain ranked for 523 keywords. By February 2024 it was 144, and it bottomed at 37 in mid-2025. Today: 113. Whatever happened to the site in early 2024 took four fifths of its search footprint with it, and it has never been rebuilt.

Critical
02

Not one line of schema, anywhere

We read the markup on the homepage, services, crowns, implants, veneers and about pages. There is no structured data at all — not even the generic WebPage tags most sites ship by default. No Dentist, no LocalBusiness, no FAQ, no reviews. Google and every AI engine are reading this site blind.

Critical
03

86 blog posts nobody can reach

Since May 2026 the practice has published 86 posts, averaging 1,500 words — a serious investment. But /blog/ returns a 404, the homepage links to none of them, and inside each article the only editorial links point to other articles. The treatment pages are reachable solely through the menu. The content exists in a closed loop, with no schema on any of it.

Critical
Other issues we found
  • No blog index exists — /blog/, /news/ and /articles/ all return 404, so the 86 posts have no entry point
  • Not one blog post carries Article, BlogPosting or FAQ schema
  • Every post carries a meta description; the homepage carries none
  • The veneers page URL is misspelled — /veeners-san-jose/ — while /veneers-san-jose/ returns a 404
  • /crowns/ and /braces/ are live pages but appear in no sitemap at all
  • The crowns page title reads "crowns – Blossom Hill Dental Center" — lowercase, and with no city in it
  • No dedicated contact or booking page — both menu items are anchors on the homepage
  • Four to five URLs compete for each branded term, splitting their own signal
  • The Google listing rates 4.1★ on 42 reviews — and appears to be unclaimed

⚡ Start Here
01
The Migration
Land the new site without losing the 94.7%.

What we'd do in Step 1

A rebuild is already underway in Lovable, which changes what Step 1 is. The risk now is not the design — it is the launch. Six brand terms at #1 carry 94.7% of all traffic, and this domain has already lost 78% of its keywords once, in February 2024. A migration done without a redirect map and schema is exactly how that happens again. Step 1 is making sure the new site inherits everything the current one earns, and fixes what it never had.

  • Build the redirect map before launch, not after. Every live URL mapped 1:1 to its new home — the six #1 brand terms, the 86 blog posts, /veeners-san-jose/, /crowns/, /braces/. This is the single action that protects 94.7% of current traffic through the switch.
  • Ship schema on the new build from day one — Dentist, LocalBusiness, MedicalProcedure, FAQPage and AggregateRating. The current site has none at all, on any page, so this is free upside rather than a rebuild cost.
  • Give the 86 posts a home. There is no blog index — /blog/ returns 404 and the homepage links to no article. Inside each post, the only editorial links go to other posts; the treatment pages are reached only through the menu. A blog index plus contextual links from each article into implants, veneers and crowns turns 86 orphans into a funnel.
  • Deepen the service pages in the new build. Implants is 456 words, crowns 212, against a market of 5,980 monthly searches. The blog posts average 1,500 words — the money pages should not be a third of that.
  • Fix the URL problems during the move. Correct the /veeners-san-jose/ spelling with a redirect from the old path, and get /crowns/ and /braces/ into the sitemap — today they are live but in no sitemap at all.
  • Give the practice a real contact and booking page. Both menu items are anchors on the homepage today, so nothing can rank for them and nothing captures booking intent on its own URL.
  • Claim the Google Business Profile and connect it to the site. It appears unclaimed, rates 4.1★ on 42 reviews, and the website does not link to it or carry any review markup.
  • Rewrite every title and meta description. The crowns page opens with a lowercase "crowns" and no city; the homepage carries no meta description at all, while every blog post does.

Why this matters: the brand is carrying this practice almost single-handedly, and a migration is the moment it is most exposed. Get the redirects and schema right and the new site launches ahead of where the old one is. Get them wrong and the February 2024 drop repeats — except this time there is no non-branded traffic underneath to cushion it.
