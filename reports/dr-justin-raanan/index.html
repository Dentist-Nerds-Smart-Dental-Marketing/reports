ISSUE 1 Critical · Usability

Treatments aren't accessible in a visible navigation menu.

The header has a single "Menu" link that opens an Elementor popup. There's no persistent top-nav, no mega-menu, no visible treatment list. Visitors who land on the homepage have to click into a popup just to see what services exist. For a high-intent visitor researching periodontists and dental implants — exactly the patients Dr. Raanan wants — that's friction they won't push through. Most will hit back and pick a competitor with a clearer site.

✓ The fix

Build a real top navigation with visible treatment categories (Dental Implants, Periodontics, Sedation, Cosmetic, etc.). The menu should be discoverable on every page, on every device, without a popup. This single change typically moves time-on-site, pages-per-visit, and conversion meaningfully.

ISSUE 2 Critical · Content / SEO

Treatment pages are too thin to rank in Beverly Hills.

The Dental Implants page — arguably the most important page on the entire site for a periodontist — comes in at roughly 700 words, and contains a duplicate FAQ ("What is the success rate of dental implants?" appears twice with virtually identical answers). Other treatment pages follow the same pattern: short, generic, AI-flavored content that reads like it was generated rather than written by an expert. In Beverly Hills, where competitors have deeper pages backed by years of authority, thin content is the single biggest reason these pages aren't ranking for the searches that drive premium-priced implant bookings.

✓ The fix

Rewrite every treatment page to at least 1,200 words — ideally 1,500–2,000 for core implant and periodontics pages. Add real case content, procedure walk-throughs, recovery info, financing FAQs, and patient stories. Remove duplicate FAQs. Each page should be the most thorough resource a Beverly Hills implant patient could find on that topic — that's what wins both Google rankings and the patient's trust.

ISSUE 3 High · Trust / Brand

Stock imagery throughout — doesn't match a premium Beverly Hills practice.

Treatment pages and supporting content lean on generic stock images (file names like sedation-dentist-Beverly-Hills-ca.png) instead of real photography of Dr. Raanan, the office, the team, or actual treatment results. For a Beverly Hills periodontist charging premium fees on $5K–$50K implant cases, stock photos undercut the entire trust signal — patients researching that kind of treatment want to see the actual doctor, the actual office, the actual equipment, and real before/after results.

✓ The fix

Remove every stock image. Replace with a professional half-day photo shoot: Dr. Raanan portraits, team shots, office interior (especially the high-end equipment — microscopes, AI radiograph software, Biolase laser), real before/after galleries (with patient consent), and short procedure video clips. Real photography is the single biggest visual upgrade for a luxury practice — and it costs less than most agencies charge for one month of SEO.

ISSUE 4 High · Local SEO

Both office addresses appear in every page footer.

The footer currently lists both the Beverly Hills clinic and the Brentwood clinic on every single page — with a typo ("San Vincente" should be "San Vicente"), and the Brentwood suite number listed as 811 is wrong (it should be 814). Mixing two NAP (Name-Address-Phone) signals on every page dilutes the local-SEO clarity for each location. Google can't tell which location each page is "about." It also creates confusion for patients trying to figure out which office to visit.

✓ The fix

Remove both office blocks from the global footer. Move the Beverly Hills address to a dedicated About / Contact page (this site's primary location). Reference the Brentwood location only on a "Locations" page or briefly in About. Fix the typo and the wrong suite number. Each location should have its own clearly-scoped page — cleaner for SEO, cleaner for patients.

ISSUE 5 High · Header Polish

Phone number in the header is missing a space — small detail, easy fix.

The phone displays as (310)205-5315 — no space between the closing parenthesis and the next digit. Easy to miss, but it reads as sloppy on a premium brand, and inconsistent with how the same number appears correctly formatted in the footer.

Currently(310)205-5315 Should be(310) 205-5315
✓ The fix

Single-character fix in the header template. Add a space between the closing parenthesis and the next digit. Apply consistently sitewide so every reference to the phone number — header, contact page, footer, schema — uses the same format.

ISSUE 6 Medium · Platform

Built on an outdated Elementor 4.0.9 build.

The page generator meta tag reveals Elementor 4.0.9 — an outdated builder version that's also the same one running the Brentwood site. Slow load times, limited schema flexibility, no AEO structure, and no inline booking flow. In Beverly Hills, where patient research on a $30K implant decision often happens on a phone over lunch, a slow page that doesn't answer the question fast loses the patient before they even reach the contact form.

✓ The fix

Modernize the technical foundation — upgrade off Elementor 4.0.9 onto a current, schema-ready stack. Keep the visual brand intact; what changes is what runs underneath it. Faster, lighter, and ready for both human visitors and AI-search extraction.

ISSUE 7 Medium · Technical SEO

No structured data / schema markup sitewide.

No Dentist schema, no Physician schema, no FAQPage schema (even though the homepage and treatment pages already contain FAQs), no MedicalProcedure markup, no AggregateRating. The FAQs are right there in the content — they just aren't structured so Google and AI search engines can pull them as answers. Same for the doctor's credentials, address, hours, and reviews. Without schema, Google has to guess what's on each page; with schema, it knows.

✓ The fix

Add the full schema suite — Dentist, Physician (for Dr. Raanan), FAQPage (the FAQs already exist), MedicalProcedure for each treatment page, AggregateRating tied to the GBP reviews — baked into the new templates. AI search engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews) lean heavily on schema to choose which sources to cite for medical questions.

ISSUE 8 Critical · Site Architecture

Only 22 indexable pages — far too thin to rank in Beverly Hills.

The full sitemap contains just 22 pages, and only 17 of those are treatment-related. For a Harvard-trained periodontist in Beverly Hills, that's a fraction of what's needed to compete. The leading periodontist sites in this market run 50–100+ pages, with dedicated pages for every implant subtype, every periodontal procedure, every patient question. Each missing page is a missed search someone in the market is doing right now.

The user noted: "As a periodontist, I know you guys do so many other treatments that are not listed on the website." Missing pages = missing keyword coverage = missing patients. The 22 pages already there get an average of 1–2 internal links each, suggesting the architecture is also under-developed for the pages that do exist.

✓ The fix

Expand the site to 50+ pages as part of the fix. Map out every treatment Dr. Raanan actually performs and give each one a dedicated, 1,200+ word page. Add pillar pages for major topic clusters (Dental Implants, Periodontics, Cosmetic Reconstruction). Build internal linking so every page is reachable in ≤2 clicks from the homepage. More relevant pages = more ranking surface area = more patients.

ISSUE 9 High · Brand Clarity

A "Meet Dr. Amir" page exists — and is more internally linked than the main doctor.

The sitemap reveals a /meet-dr-amir/ page that has 6 internal links pointing to it — more than any other page on the site. The main "Meet The Doctor" page (for Dr. Justin Raanan) has only 5. Dr. Amir doesn't appear on the homepage, doesn't appear in the brand presentation, and isn't introduced anywhere in the public-facing copy — but the page exists and gets the most internal link signal of any URL on the site.

Either Dr. Amir is a current associate who should be properly introduced on the homepage and team page (and his /meet-dr-amir/ URL kept), or he's a former associate / template leftover and the page should be removed and 301-redirected. Right now Google sees Dr. Amir as the most-linked-to person on the site, which is confusing for both search engines and patients who land on it.

✓ The fix

Investigate the Dr. Amir situation immediately. If he's a current associate: introduce him properly on the homepage, the team page, and align the brand around both doctors. If he's not active: remove the page, 301-redirect /meet-dr-amir/ to /meet-the-doctor/, and audit the rest of the site for stale references. Brand clarity is non-negotiable on a premium practice site.

ISSUE 10 High · Local SEO

Inconsistent geo-targeting — some pages target Beverly Hills, some target Los Angeles, most target neither.

The sitemap shows a split strategy that confuses Google about the practice's primary service area:

  • Targeting Los Angeles (2 pages): /los-angeles-dental-veneers/, /gum-disease-specialist-los-angeles/
  • Targeting Beverly Hills (2 pages): /root-canal-beverly-hills/, /tooth-extraction-beverly-hills/
  • No geographic target (13 pages): /orthodontics/, /dental-crown/, and most other treatment pages

For a Beverly Hills periodontist, this split signals indecision. Patients searching "dental veneers Beverly Hills" find a competitor's page before Dr. Raanan's /los-angeles-dental-veneers/. The Beverly Hills GBP listing earned the local authority — the site should compound that, not split it three ways.

✓ The fix

Standardize all geographic targeting to "Beverly Hills" across every treatment URL. Restructure to /veneers-beverly-hills/, /orthodontics-beverly-hills/, /dental-crown-beverly-hills/, etc. 301-redirect the LA-targeted pages to the new Beverly Hills equivalents. The Beverly Hills brand is the asset — every page should compound that signal, not dilute it.

ISSUE 11 High · Content Strategy

The treatment list reads as a random mix — not what a Beverly Hills periodontist should lead with.

Looking at what's actually on the site: Dental Bonding, Dental Bridge, Dental Crown, Orthodontics, Veneers, Root Canal, CAD-CAM, Holistic Dentistry, Computerized Tomography, Tooth Extraction. Many of these are general dentistry or cosmetic procedures — not the core specialist work a periodontist's practice should be built around.

Meanwhile, the periodontal procedures Dr. Raanan actually leads with (advanced perio surgery, microscope-enhanced gum grafts, sinus lifts, full-arch implants, specific implant brands and protocols, LANAP) are either missing or buried. There's also a /full-mouth-reconstruction-6/ URL with a "-6" suffix that strongly suggests duplicate versions of the same page are floating around the site.

✓ The fix

Restructure the treatment taxonomy around the three core pillars of a Harvard-trained periodontist: (1) Dental Implants & Bone Grafting, (2) Periodontal & Gum Care, (3) Advanced Aesthetic & Reconstruction. Build proper pillar-and-cluster architecture under each. Remove or de-emphasize unrelated general-dentistry pages (bonding, bridges, crowns) unless Dr. Raanan actively performs them and wants to be found for them. Clean up the duplicate full-mouth-reconstruction-6 URL. Sitemap should reflect what the practice actually does — not random pages a previous agency built.
