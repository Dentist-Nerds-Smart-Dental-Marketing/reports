⚡ Start Here
01
The Diagnosis
Find out what took the traffic, then take it back.

What we'd do in Step 1

Most audits in this series open with "rebuild the site." This one does not. The pages here are good — 2,076 words on implants, 1,846 on emergency care, the city in the title tags, 284 URLs indexed. A site like this earning 165 visits a month is not a content problem. Something changed between January 2025 and now, and until we know what, every other investment is guesswork.

  • Full backlink audit, first and before anything else. 37,827 links from 861 domains — 44 per domain — at Authority Score 8. We map the profile, identify what was built and when, and compare it against the traffic timeline. If there is a toxic cluster, a disavow goes in. This is the one task that gates everything else.
  • Pull Search Console and reconstruct the drop. Impressions, clicks and positions by date tell us whether this was an algorithm update, a manual action, a migration, or something self-inflicted. We cannot see that from outside; the practice can hand it over in minutes.
  • Refocus the content on Whittier. The biggest surviving keyword is "full coverage dental," a national insurance term, at 27.9% of traffic. The money is in "dentist whittier" (590/mo) and "whittier dentist" (480/mo), where the site ranks for neither. The pages exist — they need local targeting, not rewriting.
  • Fix the Google Business Profile. One category — "Emergency dental service" — for a practice that has placed 15,000+ implants. Add accurate primary and secondary categories, populate services, and settle who controls the listing.
  • Add the dental schema the site has never had. Dentist, LocalBusiness, MedicalProcedure, FAQPage and AggregateRating. A 4.9★ rating across 315 reviews currently appears nowhere in the markup, so neither Google nor any AI engine can use it.
  • Fix the NAP mismatch and the three weak pages. The homepage schema declares 562-567-0498 while the page and Google both show 562-696-5544. /teeth-whitening/ (318 words) and /orthodontics/ (894) need depth and the city in their titles; /general-dentistry/ needs a title that is not a copy of the homepage.

Why this matters: this practice already proved it can win at scale — 13,369 visits a month is more than any competitor in Whittier earns today. The audience, the content and the reputation are all still here. What is missing is an explanation, and recovery starts there rather than with a rebuild nobody needs.
