ISSUE 1 Critical · Reviews

The main practice GBP listing is at 106 reviews — far below where this caliber of practice should sit.

Covered in detail in the section above. The headline: the main Rifkin Raanan Google Business Profile has 106 reviews, while top BH cosmetic competitors sit at 173, 244, 413, 525, and 1,751. With the team, legacy, and patient experience already in place, the target should be 500–600 reviews on the main listing. The plan is the two-path routing strategy shown above: patients of Dr. Rodney Raanan go to his individual GBP, everyone else goes to the main practice GBP.

✓ The fix

Implement a post-visit SMS workflow with two pre-built templates — one with Dr. Rodney's review link (maps.app.goo.gl/FbCM4YDka12Zjoqf8), one with the main practice review link (maps.app.goo.gl/82BrNK3tp5Pcs9SA6). Staff picks the template based on who the patient saw. Same-day send. Pre-visit consent so the ask is expected. Optional: a printed QR card in each operatory with both links for in-office reviews. Target velocity: 8–12 new reviews per month across the two listings combined. Track weekly.

ISSUE 2 High · GBP Strategy & NAP

Multiple GBP listings share the exact same address, phone, and website — which can confuse Google.

The Rifkin Raanan practice operates with three active GBP listings at 414 N Camden Dr Suite 1280: the main Rifkin Raanan Beverly Hills Cosmetic Dentistry (106 reviews), Dr. Rodney Raanan, DDS MMSc (120 reviews), and Dr. Robert Rifkin, DDS (46 reviews). All three share the same address, the same phone number (310) 205-5300, and the same website rifkinraanan.com. This is a real NAP-consistency conflict — Google can struggle to differentiate the listings, which risks suppressing one in favor of the other or weakening all of them in the Map Pack.

This is separate from the review-routing problem. The routing strategy decides where new reviews go. The NAP conflict is about whether Google can even understand these as three distinct business entities to begin with.

✓ The fix — Dentist Nerds installs call tracking

Install dedicated call tracking numbers for each GBP listing. Each listing gets its own unique forwarding number that rings through to the main practice line at (310) 205-5300 — every call is still answered by the same front desk team, the patient experience doesn't change, and no calls are lost. But from Google's perspective, each listing now has a distinct NAP signature, which removes the duplicate-listing ambiguity. As a bonus: call tracking gives you per-listing analytics, so you can see which GBP is actually driving the phone volume that turns into bookings. Dentist Nerds handles end-to-end setup — number provisioning, GBP updates, schema markup on the site, and the call analytics dashboard.

ISSUE 3 High · Booking Flow

Appointment booking redirects to an external MyOryx URL.

Every "Schedule an Appointment" CTA on the site redirects to mychart.myoryx.com — a third-party scheduling system. This creates two compounding problems. SEO impact: sending traffic off-domain costs the site valuable on-page engagement signals (time on site, pages per session, return visits). Google reads off-domain redirects as users leaving rifkinraanan.com — which weakens the site's overall ranking signal over time. Conversion impact: third-party scheduling tools typically require more questions, more form steps, and more friction than a native flow needs — and every additional step on a booking funnel measurably lowers the percentage of patients who complete the booking. For a luxury cosmetic dentist where each consultation can represent a high-value case, even a small drop in conversion is meaningful revenue lost.

✓ The fix

Build an on-site booking flow that lives on rifkinraanan.com itself. The patient picks their doctor, the procedure of interest, preferred time, and submits — all within the Rifkin Raanan brand experience. The backend can still integrate with MyOryx, but the patient never sees the handoff. This is also a strong AI-search signal — current booking patterns are increasingly the place AI agents look to verify a practice is real and active.

ISSUE 4 Medium · Technical SEO

Schema markup gaps — the FAQ section on the homepage isn't structured for extraction.

The site has a strong FAQ section on the homepage answering questions about smile makeovers, veneers, pricing, and what makes Rifkin Raanan different. The content is there — what's missing is FAQPage schema markup wrapping it. Same applies to the doctor pages (no Physician schema), treatment pages (no MedicalProcedure schema), and the practice listing (no Dentist schema with AggregateRating). AI search engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews) lean heavily on schema to decide which sources to cite. Without it, the content is harder to surface.

✓ The fix

Add the full schema suite — Dentist (with AggregateRating tied to the GBP reviews), Physician for each doctor, FAQPage on the homepage, MedicalProcedure on every treatment page. None of this changes how the site looks to humans; it just lets Google and AI engines understand what's there. Should be a 1–2 day implementation.

ISSUE 5 Medium · Polish

Patient login and forms route to a separate ident.ws third-party platform.

Existing patient login redirects to ident.ws — same brand-break issue as the booking flow. New patient forms also live off-site. For a practice operating at this price tier, patients expect the same level of digital experience they get from a high-end concierge service. The forms work — they just look like a 2008-era dental portal.

✓ The fix

Lower-priority than the review work, but worth addressing in a Phase 2 polish pass: branded on-site patient login and form experience that matches the rest of the site's design quality. Backend can still integrate with the existing patient management system; the patient just never sees the handoff.
