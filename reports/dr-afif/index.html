Where attention is needed first

Priority ranking

Properties ordered by how much SEO attention they currently need — accounting for traffic gap vs portfolio average, brand control, link velocity, GBP review pace, and lifecycle stage.

1
Highest priority — recover lost ground

The Richmond Dental Centre

Authority 11 · 983 visits/mo · down 66% from peak · 1,283 reviews ★ 4.9

Richmond is the clearest case of regression. Authority and reputation are still in place, but page-level rankings have eroded since the July 2024 peak. Keyword count has fallen from 761 to 430.

The foundations are healthy, but the content layer that earned the long-tail rankings has aged. Over-performing on authority and review reputation, under-performing on content freshness.
2
High priority — break the brand ceiling

Urbanview Dental

Authority 9 · 435 visits/mo · brand-dependent · 386 reviews ★ 4.9

Urbanview has had four years to build non-brand visibility and has not. Its keyword footprint is less than a fifth of Parkview's despite being the same age. Almost all traffic comes from people who already know the name. Review velocity is also among the lowest in the portfolio.

Brand searches are healthy but flat. Without a discovery layer, the site cannot grow beyond the patient pool that already searches for it directly.
3
Strategic priority — define the role

View Dental Canada

Authority 8 · 135 visits/mo · family/umbrella site

View Dental Canada has the lowest authority and smallest link profile, yet sits at the top of the brand hierarchy. It also ranks for sister-clinic brand terms, splitting visibility from the location pages it should be feeding.

Is this a hub site that consolidates the brand, or a competitor to its own locations? The current configuration is doing some of each.
4
Capture the review gap

Parkview Dental

Authority 11 · 1,962 visits/mo · 418 reviews ★ 4.9

Parkview is the #1 organic site in the portfolio but only #5 on GBP review count. The 4.9★ rating is intact — the gap is volume, not quality. The mechanism that converts patient visits into reviews appears weaker at Parkview than at Waterview or Richmond.

Parkview wins search but is mid-pack on reputation. Closing the review gap would compound its already-strong organic position.
5
High potential — continue the recovery

Cityview Dental

Authority 11 · 769 visits/mo · recovering · 1,201 reviews ★ 4.8

Cityview's traffic has more than doubled since the October 2025 trough. The keyword base (739) and authority are in place — recent months suggest the recovery is underway but has not yet returned to the mid-2025 peak of 1,832 visits.

The clearest "trend up" story after Kingsview. The growth pattern needs continuity, not redirection.
6
Medium priority — own the brand fully

Waterview Dental

Authority 11 · 1,469 visits/mo · 1,472 reviews ★ 4.9

Waterview is the second-strongest site on traffic, the strongest on reputation, and the only clinic ranking top 3 for "toronto dentist." Its one open issue is brand defence — "waterview dental" sits at #2, not #1.

Waterview is performing. The asterisk is brand control on its highest-volume own-name query.
7
Maintain & nurture — newest site

Yorkview Dental

Authority 9 · 126 visits/mo · 5 months old · 135 reviews ★ 4.7

Yorkview is the youngest site in the portfolio. The trajectory is normal for a site that began ranking in December 2025 — brand searches are owned, most other rankings sit beyond position 30. Review velocity is excellent for the age.

Too young to draw conclusions from. The next six months of trend data will be more useful than the current snapshot.
8
Compound — protect the growth curve

Kingsview Dental

Authority 10 · 583 visits/mo · set new peak in March · 710 reviews ★ 4.8

Kingsview is the clearest momentum story in the portfolio. It set a new traffic peak in March 2026 — the only site to do so this year — and has compounded steadily since launch. Review pace is also strong.

Kingsview's growth pattern is working. The risk is interrupting it.
Portfolio-level issues

Issues observed across the portfolio

The three patterns that show up across every site in the portfolio — followed by site-specific issues. Each is described in plain English: what's happening, where it shows up, and why it matters.

1
Thin content on treatment pages — every site

Across all 8 properties, 50+ procedure pages are indexed but generate zero monthly visits. Implants, Invisalign, whitening, root canals, periodontist, veneers, TMJ — the URLs exist but lack content depth (description, process, cost ranges, photos). Patient demand exists; content does not. This is the single biggest portfolio-wide gap.

Issue
2
No FAQs or AI-visibility optimization — every site

FAQ pages exist on most clinic sites (View Dental Canada has 20 Q&A pairs on its homepage, Parkview has 15 on /faqs, etc.) — but none are optimized for AI search engines. Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Claude can't surface those answers. Zero FAQ blocks on the actual treatment pages where they would help patients convert.

Issue
3
Treatment-page conversion layout needs rebuild — every site

Treatment pages across the portfolio are missing conversion-driving elements: prominent "Book Online" CTA above the fold, before/after photo galleries, transparent cost ranges, FAQ accordion sections, and embedded patient testimonials. Cityview's Invisalign cost page is the only template close to working — replicate it portfolio-wide.

Issue
4
Review velocity varies 3–4× across clinics of the same age

Parkview (418) and Urbanview (386) trail Waterview (1,472) and Richmond (1,283) by 3–4× on Google review count despite identical site age. Seven similar clinics with similar patient volumes — review velocity shouldn't vary this widely. Review velocity is a local-pack ranking signal.

Issue
5
Umbrella site cannibalizes sister-clinic brand searches

viewdentalcanada.com currently ranks for "kingsview dental," "urbanview dental," and "yorkview dental" — splitting brand visibility three ways. When Google returns multiple owned sites for the same brand query, click distribution is diluted.

Issue
6
Richmond Dental Centre lost 66% of organic traffic since July 2024

Reputation and backlinks are intact (1,283 reviews, 725 backlinks) — but page-level rankings have decayed steadily over 21 months. Keyword count fell from 761 to 430. Content freshness decay relative to improving competitor pages.

Other Key Findings · Parkview Dental

●
Homepage carries 73% of organic traffic

301 keywords route to the homepage; the next-highest page (blog/quit-that-ice-chewing-habit) drives only 10% of total traffic. Heavy reliance on a single entry point.

⚠
Blog content over-indexed vs service pages

Informational blog content drives ~21% of traffic; service pages drive only 4%. Patient-acquisition keywords like "veneers toronto," "dental emergencies," and "Invisalign" need stronger on-page work and internal links to convert authority into commercial traffic.

⚠
5 Squarespace placeholder URLs indexed

URLs containing "blog-post-title-one-..." are default Squarespace template paths that have been indexed by Google. These need to be removed, noindex'd, or redirected to relevant destinations.

↓
Service-page internal-link density is weak

The /procedures-1/ pages receive few internal links from blog content or the homepage. Internal links are how authority flows through a site — weak link density limits how Google distributes ranking signals to service pages.
