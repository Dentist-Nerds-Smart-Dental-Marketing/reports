Updates Issue 01 AI Search Readiness

The site looks great — but it's invisible to ChatGPT, Perplexity & Google AI Overviews

What's happening

Strong design, zero AI-readable signal

The site itself looks great — clean design, clear navigation, professional photography, strong brand presentation. The gap is purely technical readiness for AI search.

No LocalBusiness, MedicalProcedure, FAQPage, AggregateRating, or Article schema is deployed anywhere on the site, and there's no llms.txt file at the root telling AI crawlers which pages represent the canonical knowledge base.

AI engines read schema to pick up procedure data, provider credentials, ratings, and FAQ answers — and increasingly use llms.txt as a routing signal for the content they cite. The 540+ reviews, the "Family Favorites" awards, and every cost-guide answer are all invisible to those channels.

How we fix it

AI-readiness deployment package

  • Publish an llms.txt file at the root pointing AI crawlers to canonical treatment pages, provider bios, and cost guides.
  • Add LocalBusiness + AggregateRating schema on the homepage and each location page, pulling in the 540+ review count and the "Family Favorites" awards.
  • Layer MedicalProcedure schema on every implant, cosmetic, sedation, and orthodontic treatment page.
  • Layer Article + FAQPage schema on the top-traffic cost-guide pages so the existing content becomes AI-quotable.
  • Add Person / Physician schema on each doctor's bio page.
  • Validate everything through Google's Rich Results Test and Schema.org validator before launch.
02
New finding Content strategy

The blog is cannibalizing the homepage and treatment-page keywords

What's happening

Authority flowing to pages that don't convert

The homepage leads with three primary sections: Full Mouth Dental Implants (All-on-4 / All-on-X), Sedation Dentistry, and Smile Makeovers. These are the exact topics the cost-guide engine ranks for:

/zirconia-bridge-price-guide/ 3,154 visits · /full-mouth-dental-implants-cost/ 2,414 · /zirconia-full-arch-cost/ 1,638 · /smile-makeover-cost-guide/ 205.

Google is reading the blog as the canonical answer for procedure queries that should land on the homepage or the matching treatment pages. ~16,000 monthly visits flow to informational pages with low transactional intent, while the homepage gets 1,109 and the commercial service pages capture under 250 combined. The content engine is too good at its informational job — and starving the pages that book patients.

How we fix it

Redirect authority back to treatment pages

  • Pair-audit each high-traffic cost-guide page against its matching treatment page (implants cost ↔ /dental-implants/, zirconia full-arch cost ↔ /dental-implants/all-on-4-dental-implants/, smile makeover cost ↔ /cosmetic-dentistry/smile-makeover/).
  • Move the deeper transactional content — pricing examples, consultation CTAs, provider info, before-and-afters — onto the treatment pages. Keep cost guides focused on the informational answer.
  • Add prominent internal links from each cost-guide page to its parent treatment page, with descriptive anchor text Google can read as a topic relationship.
  • Restructure the treatment-page hierarchy so each is a clear topical hub the cost guides feed up into.
  • Apply rel="canonical" selectively where two pages are competing for the same query, consolidating ranking signals on the page that converts.
  • Add location-specific CTAs and Baton Rouge/Metairie pricing context on every cost-guide page so informational traffic has a path to a consultation.
03
New action Brand exposure

Earn local press mentions to feed AI brand-exposure signals

What's happening

AI models weight brands by mention volume

AI engines — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, Copilot — rank brands partly by mention volume and the authority of the sources doing the mentioning, not just by traditional SEO signals.

The more the "Pearl Dental Group" entity appears in trusted, third-party contexts, the more often it surfaces when those models answer queries like "best dentist in Baton Rouge" or "all-on-4 implants Louisiana."

Right now, Pearl Dental's brand authority lives almost entirely on its own domain. The 540+ reviews are real, the four "Family Favorites" wins are real — but neither shows up as third-party mentions in the news domains AI engines weight most heavily.

How we fix it

Quarterly press & community PR cadence

  • Build a media list of Baton Rouge outlets (The Advocate, WBRZ, 225 Magazine, Baton Rouge Business Report) and Metairie / New Orleans-area media (Nola.com, Gambit, Biz New Orleans).
  • Quarterly press releases tied to "Family Favorites" wins, new technology installs, provider milestones, charitable initiatives, community-event sponsorships, and patient-impact stories.
  • Pitch local lifestyle & parenting features — the Bruni family-dentist angle is a natural fit for Baton Rouge Parents beyond the annual awards issue.
  • Partner with LSU, the Greater Baton Rouge Dental Association, and local charities for co-branded community programs that earn linked news coverage.
  • Track AI visibility monthly — prompt-test "best dentist in Baton Rouge," "all-on-4 Louisiana," and similar queries across ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews to measure brand surface as the press cadence builds.
  • Mirror earned mentions into schema via mentions and subjectOf properties on the homepage LocalBusiness entity so the press wins compound on-site too.
04
New finding Provider visibility

Dr. Rachel Nery is on the site nav but missing everywhere it counts

What's happening

A fourth provider with no digital footprint

Dr. Rachel Nery appears in the main site navigation as a Pearl Dental provider — but the supporting infrastructure is missing.

There's no indexed team bio at /about-us/meet-the-team/dr-rachel-nery/, no individual Healthgrades or Vitals profile, and no LinkedIn or Louisiana Dental Association directory link tied back to pearldentalgroup.com.

Missing provider pages mean missing E-E-A-T signal, missing Person/Physician schema, and missing surface area for any AI Overview or ChatGPT answer that names individual providers when responding to "best dentist in Baton Rouge" queries. The other three doctors are well-covered — Dr. Nery's presence needs to match.

How we fix it

Full provider buildout for Dr. Nery

  • Build a complete team bio page at /about-us/meet-the-team/dr-rachel-nery/ matching the depth of the existing three doctor pages — credentials, training, photography, treatment focus, patient stories.
  • Layer Person + Physician schema on her page and on the homepage doctors block.
  • Claim or build individual external profiles — Healthgrades, Vitals, LinkedIn, Louisiana Dental Association directory — with consistent NAP and a link back to her on-site bio.
  • Add her to the "Meet the Doctors" submenu at parity with the other three providers (currently inconsistent across navigation states).
  • Update the homepage doctors section to show all four providers, and the meta description / OG tags where applicable.
  • Cross-link her bio from the relevant treatment pages she practices (so the new authority feeds the commercial pages, not just the bio).
05
New finding Geographic footprint

Metairie isn't in the homepage's primary signals — it competes as an orphan

What's happening

Root-level geo signal is Baton Rouge only

The Veterans Blvd / Metairie location pages have meaningful potential but currently produce a fraction of the Baton Rouge offices' traffic.

Compounding the gap: the homepage H1 reads "Cosmetic And Implant Dentistry — Baton Rouge, LA" and the meta title reads "Pearl Dental Group | Trusted Dentist in Baton Rouge." Neither mentions Metairie or the New Orleans metro.

Google's primary geographic signal for the entire site is Baton Rouge — the Metairie location pages are competing as orphans without root-level reinforcement. Every external link to the homepage is voting for Baton Rouge authority, not for a multi-metro practice.

How we fix it

Re-anchor the site as a multi-metro practice

  • Rewrite the homepage H1 to include both metros — e.g. "Cosmetic and Implant Dentistry — Baton Rouge & Metairie, LA."
  • Rewrite the meta title and OG title to signal both markets to search engines and social previews.
  • Update the meta description to name both Baton Rouge and Metairie service areas.
  • Add a Metairie-focused content block to the homepage (location card, Metairie-specific testimonial, or quick-link to the Veterans Blvd page).
  • Build out Metairie-specific service pages — Metairie implants, Metairie cosmetic dentistry, Metairie emergency dentistry — with locally-targeted FAQ content and pricing context.
  • Layer a second LocalBusiness entity for the Metairie location with its own NAP, hours, and aggregate rating drawn from Metairie-specific reviews.
  • Citation & backlink work in the New Orleans metro to establish Metairie as its own market entity rather than a Baton Rouge spillover.
06
New finding UX & crawlability

Navigation routing & menu QA — small bugs accumulating into real friction

What's happening

Broken anchors, mis-routed links, duplicate items

The global "Patient Center" link in the primary navigation routes to /metairie/patient-center/ — a location-scoped URL — sending Baton Rouge visitors to the wrong office's content.

The mobile menu splits "Porcelain Veneers And Lumineers" into two separate clickable items. Several submenu parents — "Meet the Doctors," "Periodontics," "General Dentistry" — point to placeholder anchors (#) or off-topic URLs (the General Dentistry parent currently routes to the Porcelain Veneers page).

Each one is small. Together they add up to UX friction for patients and confused crawl interpretation of the site's topical hierarchy.

How we fix it

Full navigation audit & cleanup

  • Route the global "Patient Center" link to a top-level /patient-center/ URL (not the Metairie-scoped one), with location-specific sub-pages underneath.
  • Fix the mobile menu — collapse "Porcelain Veneers And" + "Lumineers" into a single item linking to the correct page.
  • Replace placeholder # anchors on parent menu items with either real landing pages or proper expand/collapse behavior.
  • Re-route the "General Dentistry" parent off the Porcelain Veneers page and onto a real General Dentistry hub page.
  • Standardize menus across desktop & mobile — Dr. Nery currently appears inconsistently, and other items disagree between viewports.
  • Add breadcrumb schema so the corrected hierarchy is machine-readable.
  • Submit the updated sitemap to Search Console after the menu cleanup to refresh Google's understanding of the site's topical structure.
