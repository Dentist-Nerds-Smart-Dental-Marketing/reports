Executive summary

Other Key Findings · What Needs Improvement

⚠
Service-page internal-link density is weak

The homepage holds 93% of organic traffic but doesn't surface a service-grid block with descriptive anchor text. Visitors land on the homepage and don't navigate deeper, and authority can't flow to the service pages without paths to follow.

⚠
22+ legacy URLs still 301-redirecting through old site structure

Backlink crawl reveals dozens of old URL patterns (/orthodontics/, /preventive_dentistry/, /cosmetic_dentistry/, /dental_implants/, /pediatric_dentistry/, /endodontics/root_canals/, /periodontics/, /oral_surgery/, /appointments/, /teeth-whitening, plus 12 more) all 301-redirecting to current pages. Each redirect chain leaks crawl budget and a fraction of link equity.

⚠
Traffic is volatile with no clear trend

12-month range: 150 (May 25) → 296 (Mar 26 peak) → 170 (Apr 26) → 203 (current). The site oscillates between 150–300 visits with no compounding growth. Strong months aren't sustained, and the lift comes from brand or keyword volatility rather than systematic improvement.

⚠
Two practicing doctors but only one has a profile page

Dr. Myriam V. Falcon has a dedicated page (/myriam-v-falcon--dds); Dr. David Garate does not have a similar standalone profile page indexed. The /meet-the-doctor URL is generic. Doctor-name searches won't reliably surface the practice for prospects researching Dr. Garate specifically.

⚠
Service pages missing key conversion elements

The dental-implants page is well-written (1,200+ words, FAQs, CTAs) but lacks embedded patient testimonials, before/after gallery, and cost ranges. Same pattern applies across the service set. Visitors who do reach service pages don't see the social-proof or financial-clarity elements that drive consultation requests.

⚠
Content footprint is thin — 33 pages, 282 ranking keywords

Sitemap shows only 33 total pages and the site ranks for 282 keywords — below every nearby competitor (292 to 428). Dentistry is a high-intent vertical with dozens of treatment, sub-treatment, and patient-question queries; the current page set is far too small to compete on the full surface. Need more service depth, sub-topic pages, FAQ pages, and a productive blog to widen the ranking footprint.

⚠
Blog post bylines literally say "SEO"

At least one blog author is listed as "SEO" — a placeholder string instead of a real person. Google's E-E-A-T guidelines (experience, expertise, authoritativeness, trust) lean heavily on named, credentialed authors, especially in medical and health verticals. Replace every "SEO" byline with the actual author — ideally one of the doctors with a short bio and credentials linked to their profile page.

⚠
Service-page layouts need a conversion-focused rebuild

Beyond the content itself, the visual layout on service pages doesn't guide a visitor toward booking. Treatment content needs to be paired with conversion blocks at consistent scroll intervals — phone CTA, before/after gallery, embedded testimonial, financing snippet, and an inline booking widget — so any scroll position contains a clear next step. Right now the pages read as informational rather than as a path to a consultation.

⚠
ADA accessibility — contrast failures and no accessibility widget

Light orange and yellow accents paired with white text fall below WCAG AA contrast minimums in several places — both a legal-exposure issue under ADA Title III for healthcare sites and a real usability problem for older patients. Two-part fix: tighten color usage so all text and interactive elements meet contrast standards, and install an ADA accessibility widget (UserWay, accessiBe, or equivalent) so users can adjust contrast, font size, and reading mode themselves.

⚠
Pull orange back to accents — current background use works against the brand

Orange is shown to elevate heart rate and visual urgency — useful for sale buttons and hover states, but counterproductive across large background panels of a healthcare site, where the goal is calm and trust. Pull orange into a smaller role (hover states, small highlight chips, CTA accents) and let calmer base colors carry the larger surfaces. This also makes the contrast fixes above easier.
