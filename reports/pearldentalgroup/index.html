ISSUE 1 Critical · Site Strategy

One website is trying to serve two different markets — Baton Rouge and Metairie.

Pearl Dental Group operates three offices: Perkins Rd and Jones Creek Rd in Baton Rouge (75 miles inland), and Veterans Blvd in Metairie (suburb of New Orleans). Treating these as one SEO entity forces every treatment page to either pick a city or stay generic — neither of which ranks well for local-intent searches. For example: the /emergency-dentistry/ page targets "Baton Rouge, LA" — but a New Orleans patient searching "emergency dentist Metairie" won't find Pearl Dental ranking for that. Same problem in reverse for Baton Rouge-specific terms when the Metairie content fights for attention.

✓ The recommendation — split into two websites

Build one website for the two Baton Rouge offices (Perkins Rd + Jones Creek Rd, since they share a metro and competitive field) and a separate website for the Metairie office (730 Veterans Blvd). Each site gets its own clean geo-targeting, its own keyword strategy, its own backlink profile, and its own GBP integration. This is the single biggest lever Pearl Dental Group can pull for long-term local SEO growth. The brand and visual identity stay the same across both sites — what changes is that each one is optimized for the right city.

ISSUE 2 Critical · Cannibalization

Blogs are competing with the homepage and treatment pages for the same keywords.

Several blog posts target the exact same keywords the homepage and treatment pages are trying to rank for in Baton Rouge — which means Google has to pick which page to show, and often shows the wrong one (or neither well). Examples we found:

  • /pearl-dental-group-the-pinnacle-of-cosmetic-dentistry-in-baton-rouge/ — competes with /cosmetic-dentistry/
  • /revolutionize-your-smile-with-premium-dental-implants-in-baton-rouge/ — competes with /dental-implants/
  • /enhancing-your-smile-with-porcelain-veneers-in-baton-rouge-pearl-dental-group/ — competes with /cosmetic-dentistry/porcelain-veneers-lumineers/
  • /the-differences-between-implant-supported-dentures-and-all-on-4-dental-implants-in-baton-rouge-la/ — competes with /dental-implants/all-on-4-dental-implants/

This is cannibalization — both the blog and the treatment page are fighting for the same keyword. Google can't tell which one to show first, so it dilutes the ranking signal for both. The treatment page (the page that converts) loses ground to the blog (the page that doesn't).

✓ The fix

Audit every blog against the treatment pages. For overlapping topics: either 301-redirect the blog into the treatment page (combining content + link equity), or refocus the blog onto a long-tail informational angle the treatment page doesn't cover (e.g., a recovery-experience post, financing-comparison post, FAQ deep-dive). The treatment page should always rank first for the core money keyword; the blog should support it, not fight it.

ISSUE 3 Critical · Traffic Intent

Most of the traffic is informational research, not patients ready to book.

Covered in detail in the section above. Pearl Dental Group's top traffic-driving pages are price-research blogs ("zirconia bridge", "full fixed arch implant cost", "full mouth dental implants cost") and symptom-research blogs ("my crown fell out", "side of tooth decay", "black gums"). These pull big traffic numbers but they're nationally-distributed informational queries. Someone in Houston researching zirconia bridge prices will never book at Pearl Dental Group. Meanwhile the homepage and treatment pages — the ones designed to convert local searches into bookings — are getting smaller slices of attention.

✓ The fix

Don't kill the informational blogs — they build authority. But rebalance the SEO investment so the homepage and treatment pages get the priority for local-intent terms: "dentist Baton Rouge," "dental implants Baton Rouge," "veneers Baton Rouge," "emergency dentist Metairie," etc. Strengthen the homepage and treatment pages with Baton Rouge-specific content, local schema markup, internal links from blog → treatment page (not the reverse), and clean URL geo-modifiers. The goal: keep the big traffic and add more of the right traffic on top.

ISSUE 4 High · Content Quality

ChatGPT URLs and UTMs are leaking into published page titles.

We found published pages where the URL itself contains a ChatGPT UTM parameter — for example:

/transform-your-smile-with-confidence-dental-implants-baton-rouge-for-lasting-results/?utm_source=chatgpt.com

This is a strong signal that the page was copy-pasted from a ChatGPT response without removing the tracking parameter — the URL slug is the AI-generated headline phrasing, and the UTM is the source attribution ChatGPT itself adds. The page reads like AI output too (long, generic, "transformative" / "revolutionize" / "pinnacle of excellence" filler language). Google increasingly down-ranks pages that read as low-effort AI-generated content, especially when the published URL itself reveals the source.

✓ The fix

Audit every URL with ?utm_source=chatgpt.com in it and clean it up — strip the UTM, rewrite the slug to a clean keyword-focused phrase, and revise the body copy to read like it was written by the practice rather than a generative AI. AI is a useful drafting tool, but the published version has to be edited by a human to remove generic language, source UTMs, and the obvious AI-tells. This is also a good time to merge or 301-redirect the AI-generated blogs that overlap with Issue 2 (cannibalization).

ISSUE 5 High · GBP NAP Conflict

Two Google Business Profiles share the same address and phone at the Perkins location.

At 7017 Perkins Rd, Pearl Dental Group operates two separate Google Business Profile listings that share the same phone number:

Same building, same phone, slight address variation. Google's local algorithm uses Name + Address + Phone (NAP) to differentiate businesses — when two listings overlap this closely, Google can struggle to tell them apart, which risks suppressing one in the Map Pack or weakening both. This is the same problem we identified on the Rifkin Raanan audit.

✓ The fix — Dentist Nerds installs call tracking

Install a dedicated call-tracking number on one of the two listings. The new number rings through to the same front desk at (225) 277-3311, so no calls are lost and the patient experience doesn't change. But from Google's perspective, each listing now has a unique NAP signature, which removes the duplicate-listing ambiguity. Bonus: call-tracking analytics let you see which listing actually drives the phone volume that turns into bookings. Dentist Nerds handles end-to-end setup: number provisioning, GBP updates, schema markup on the website, and the analytics dashboard.

ISSUE 6 High · Location → GBP Links

Location pages on the site aren't linking to the right Google Business Profiles.

Two location-page issues that confuse the link between the website and Google:

  • Jones Creek Rd & Perkins Rd location pages link to the same GBP. Each office should link to its own dedicated GBP (Pearl Dental Group & Dental Implant Center for Perkins · Pearl Dental Group - Jones Creek Rd. for Jones Creek). Right now Google can't cleanly associate each location page on the site with its matching GBP.
  • Veterans Blvd (Metairie) location page links to just an address, not the GBP. The page should link directly to the Metairie Pearl Dental Group GBP — that's how Google associates the website page with the local business profile and ranks it for "dentist Metairie" type searches.
✓ The fix

Update each location page so it links directly to its own GBP. Use the Google Maps "embed code" or the GBP profile URL — not just a static address. This is one of the strongest local-SEO signals because it tells Google "this URL on this website represents this business at this Map Pack listing." Easy to fix, meaningful uplift.

ISSUE 7 High · Internal Linking

Internal links contain ChatGPT UTMs and link to the wrong destinations.

On the Dental Implants page, the brand name "Pearl Dental Group" is hyperlinked back to the homepage with a ?utm_source=chatgpt parameter attached. Same root cause as Issue 4 (AI-generated content not edited before publishing), and the same impact: internal links that pass UTM parameters to the homepage muddy the analytics, create duplicate-URL crawling waste for Google, and signal low-quality content management.

More broadly: internal linking on the site needs a strategic audit. The treatment pages should be the destinations that receive the most internal link equity (because they're the pages that convert) — not the homepage. Most blogs should link down to treatment pages, not across to other blogs or up to the homepage.

✓ The fix

Crawl the site, identify every internal link with a UTM parameter, and strip them. Then audit the link architecture: each treatment page should receive 8–15 contextual internal links from related blogs and from the homepage. Each blog should have 1–3 links pointing down to its parent treatment page. Clean links, clean equity flow, no tracking-parameter pollution.

ISSUE 8 Medium · Platform

The site appears to be built on Elementor — which tends to run slower and produce bloated code.

The page generator tag indicates Elementor as the underlying builder. Elementor-built sites tend to load more slowly than purpose-built ones because the page builder outputs a lot of extra wrapper divs, inline styles, and helper scripts that aren't strictly needed for what's on the page. The result is a heavier site — slower to load, harder for Google to crawl efficiently, and more complicated to maintain or extend with cleaner schema and per-location templates.

✓ The fix

This is a lower priority than the strategic issues above — the current setup is producing meaningful traffic, so an immediate rebuild isn't necessary. But when the two-website split (Issue 1) happens, it's the right moment to migrate both sites onto a leaner, faster, schema-ready stack with cleaner per-location templates, better Core Web Vitals, and code that doesn't drag the rest of the SEO work down. Plan it as part of the split, not as a separate project.

ISSUE 9 Medium · Technical SEO

Schema markup is incomplete sitewide.

No comprehensive Dentist schema with proper LocalBusiness tagging per location, no Physician schema on doctor pages, no FAQPage schema even where homepage content already includes FAQs, no MedicalProcedure schema on treatment pages, no AggregateRating tied to the GBP review counts. AI search engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews) lean heavily on schema markup to decide which sources to cite for medical questions — without it, the content is harder to surface, regardless of how good it is.

✓ The fix

Add the full schema suite: Dentist + LocalBusiness per location (one for each of the three offices, with the correct NAP), Physician on each doctor page, FAQPage wherever FAQs already exist, MedicalProcedure on each treatment page, and AggregateRating tied to the GBP review counts. This is a 1–2 day implementation that produces a meaningful uplift in AI-search visibility — and becomes especially valuable when the two-website split happens, because each new site gets its own clean schema layer from day one.
