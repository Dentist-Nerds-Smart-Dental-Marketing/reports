ISSUE 01 · SITEWIDE Critical · Brand Contamination

"Smile Studio Los Angeles" — another practice's brand — appears across the site

References to "Smile Studio Los Angeles Dentists" and "Smile Studio" appear on Dr. Raanan's bio page, the General Dentistry page, the Cosmetic Dentistry page, and likely others. The site is wearing another practice's brand by accident.

📌 Evidence — Dr. Justin Raanan bio page

"Smile Studio has been on the leading edge of that transformation from using computer-aided manufacturing (CAD/CAM) design techniques..."

📌 Evidence — General Dentistry page CTA

"Come in for a consultation and find out all about the best porcelain dental veneers in Los Angeles. Let Smile Studio Los Angeles Dentists help you Own Your Smile™."

✓ The fix

This isn't a find-and-replace problem — it tells us the entire site was built from another practice's template and never fully customized. The Cosmetic Dentistry page even lists pediatric procedures (Pulp Therapy, Space Maintainers) under "cosmetic services." A rebuild solves this cleanly. Patching means auditing every page, every section, every footer.

ISSUE 02 Critical · Keyword Cannibalization

Blog posts are competing against (and cannibalizing) the service pages

Several pieces of content are published as blog posts (og:type: article) when they should be service pages. When two URLs on the same domain target the same keywords, Google has to pick one — and rankings split, suppressing both.

✓ The fix

Audit every blog post. Where a post targets the same intent as a service page, fold its content INTO the service page and 301-redirect. Keep blog posts for educational, top-of-funnel content (like the tartar article that works). A rebuild lets us design the content architecture properly from day one.

ISSUE 03 · SITEWIDE Critical · Broken Links

Broken & placeholder links across the doctor's bio and other key pages

On Dr. Justin Raanan's bio page, multiple "Read more" and credential links are broken or point to about:blank. The malformed link inside the cosmetic blog post is another example. The "View All Services" button on the homepage points to # (nowhere). The Harvard / USC / ADA / Alpha Omega credentialing logos all share one mashed-together URL string instead of linking individually to verification sources.

Cosmetic blog internal link: http://Best Cosmetic Dentistry...https://dentalcareofbrentwood.com/
Multiple "Read more" links on doctor bio pages: broken
✓ The fix

Broken links hurt SEO directly — Google interprets them as a quality-of-maintenance signal. Patients researching credentials lose trust when "Harvard School of Dental Medicine" doesn't link to the actual Harvard page. A full link audit on the current site would touch every page; rebuilding lets us build URLs correctly from the start.

ISSUE 04 Critical · UX + SEO

Services don't exist in the main menu

The "MENU" button opens a popup with only top-level links. Invisalign, Implants, Veneers, Laser Dentistry, Gum Grafting, Sedation, Pediatric — none appear in navigation. Patients have to scroll the homepage and click through categories. Search engines treat unlinked service pages as low-priority, which is one reason Invisalign and Laser Dentistry show zero organic traffic.

✓ The fix

A modern mega-menu organized by specialty: Cosmetic · Restorative · Periodontal · General · Orthodontics · Pediatric · Sedation. Every service linked from the global header. This is foundational architecture — easier to design from scratch than retrofit into Elementor.

ISSUE 05 Critical · SEO + Conversion

"Appointment" and "Emergencies" send patients off your domain

Both header buttons link to mychart.myoryx.com. The moment a patient clicks, they leave the site entirely — taking SEO signals, analytics, and remarketing opportunities with them. The buttons also visually break on hover. One reason the Authority Score sits at 9/100.

✓ The fix

An on-site /book page that captures the lead, then embeds MyOryx or hands off to the front desk. Keep visitors on the domain through conversion. Add UTM tracking. Modern dental sites handle this natively — Elementor templates don't.

ISSUE 06 · SITEWIDE High · Layout Bugs

Layout breaks across service pages — phone number splits into two lines

On the General Dentistry page (and others), the phone number wraps onto two lines because the "Call Now (310) 826-8911 / Book an Appointment" line isn't wrapped in a proper non-breaking element. Hard-coded line breaks in the page builder also misalign content across viewports. These are symptoms of a page built one-off in Elementor instead of from reusable, responsive components.

✓ The fix

Modern component-based templates make this impossible — a "phone CTA" component renders correctly on every page automatically. Fixing in Elementor means editing each page individually.

ISSUE 07 High · Brand + AEO

Stock photography across every treatment page

Image filenames like pexels-rb-audiovisual-1996887-scaled.jpg confirm Pexels stock photos sitewide. A luxury practice that teaches at Harvard is hiding its real credibility — actual office, actual team, actual equipment, actual before/after results — behind generic imagery. AI search engines also weight original visuals.

✓ The fix

Half-day professional photo shoot: doctors, team, office, equipment (X-NAV, Waterlase, in-house lab), real before/after gallery. Integrating new photography into a rebuild is straightforward; retrofitting it into Elementor's existing image blocks is painful.

ISSUE 08 High · AI Search

FAQs aren't structured for ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google AI

The homepage has strong FAQ content — but no FAQPage schema, no semantic markup, and questions are phrased as internal speak ("Can a Periodontist help?") instead of how patients search ("How much do dental implants cost in Brentwood?"). AI engines can't extract it cleanly, so they cite competitors instead.

✓ The fix

A rebuild ships with FAQ schema sitewide, patient-language questions, and FAQs on each service page instead of stacked on the homepage. The AEO framework gets built in — not bolted on.

ISSUE 09 High · SEO Strategy

All traffic is on a blog post that doesn't book patients

The tartar article pulls ~390 visits/month — and almost none of them convert. No contextual CTA back to cleanings or periodontal service pages. Meanwhile Invisalign and Laser Dentistry rank for keywords but get zero traffic.

✓ The fix

Rebuild the funnel: contextual CTAs on educational content linking to the right service pages. Add 1–2 long-tail articles per month following the same pattern. Service pages need to be rebuilt anyway — a fresh site lets us model the entire path from "tartar question" to "booked cleaning" intentionally.

ISSUE 10 High · Strategy

The practice is competing against its own second website

Semrush flags drjustinraanan.com as a direct organic competitor. The header logo also links to drrodneyraanan.com. Multiple Raanan-family practice sites are competing for the same keywords — Google has to choose, and rankings split.

✓ The fix

Pick dentalcareofbrentwood.com as the primary domain. 301-redirect drjustinraanan.com. Remove the secondary logo. Domain consolidation needs to happen as part of the rebuild migration.

ISSUE 11 Medium · Schema

Schema markup is missing or incomplete

No Dentist schema, no Physician schema for each doctor, no FAQPage on FAQs, no MedicalProcedure on treatment pages, no AggregateRating to surface the 50+ five-star reviews in Google's rich results.

✓ The fix

A modern rebuild ships with all relevant schema baked into the templates — every doctor page automatically gets Physician schema, every service page automatically gets MedicalProcedure schema. Add-on plugins in WordPress/Elementor get half of this right.

ISSUE 12 Medium · Off-Page

Domain authority is at 9/100 — well below the local benchmark

The domain has 2,970 backlinks from 428 referring domains, but Authority Score sits at 9/100. The brand reputation (Harvard, 50+ five-star reviews, Best Dentist Brentwood 2025) isn't reflected online because the site isn't structured to capture and signal that authority.

✓ The fix

A clean rebuild with proper schema, citation consistency, real photography, and AEO structure will let Google understand the practice's actual authority. Authority score follows from there over 3–6 months.

Our Game Plan

How we'd grow the practice — step by step.

We don't believe in flashy launches that flame out. The plan starts with a strong technical foundation, then layers ongoing engines on top — each one compounding the last over months and years.

⚡ Start Here
01
The Foundation
Rebuild the website from the ground up.

What we'd do in Step 1

This is the heaviest lift — and the precondition for everything that comes next. We rebuild the entire website with a strong technical and on-page SEO foundation. Without this layer in place, the work in the engines below won't compound the way it should.

  • Rebuild the website. Start fresh on a modern platform with a clean visual design, premium Brentwood positioning, and a structure that reflects the four-doctor multi-specialty practice.
  • Fix every layout issue and broken link. Clean URLs, proper redirects, no more about:blank placeholders, no more malformed links inside blog posts.
  • Lock in a strong SEO structure. Proper heading hierarchy, semantic HTML, clean internal linking, mega-menu navigation, Core Web Vitals optimized from day one.
  • Add FAQs to every treatment page — written in patient language, structured for both SEO and AI search extraction.
  • Schema markup on every page — Dentist, Physician, FAQPage, MedicalProcedure, AggregateRating. Set up so every treatment page and informational page gets the right schema automatically.
  • Blog-by-blog audit. Read each post, identify cannibalization (where a blog competes with a treatment page), and either fold the post into the treatment page or rewrite/redirect. Stops confusing Google about which page to rank.
  • Real photography — doctors, team, office, equipment, before/afters. Zero stock photos.

Why this matters: a strong foundation is what every other channel — reviews, backlinks, content, AI search — needs to actually compound. Skipping it means the rest of the work leaks instead of stacks.
